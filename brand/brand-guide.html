What changed, and why

  • Kept: every symbolCrown, quartered shield, quill, open book, graduation cap, atom, ribbon, and the green and gold. Nothing families know was taken away.
  • Added: the olive branchesThe old crest never referred to the school's name. Two olive sprigs now frame the shield, an old symbol of wisdom, peace and patient growth.
  • Redrawn icons with one handThe four quarter icons were generic clip-art in four different styles. They are now drawn with matching line weights, so they look like one family.
  • A hidden detailThe nucleus of the atom is an olive. You don't notice it until someone points it out, and then you can't stop seeing it.
  • Proper heraldic structureThe shield has a gold border with an inner keyline, the cross meets that keyline cleanly, and the crown sits on a band that follows the curve of the shield.
  • A real ribbonThe banner now folds behind itself into swallowtails with a shaded underside, and the lettering follows the curve precisely.
  • Custom-set lettering"OLIVE SCHOOLS" is set in Fraunces, a warm old-style serif, with each letter placed on the curve and converted to outlines, so it looks the same on every device.
  • No phone number in the logoContact details date quickly and make a crest look like an advert. The number lives on the website, letterheads and posters instead.
  • Deeper, richer coloursThe lemon yellow became a warmer gold (#E7B649) and the green became a deeper school green (#0F4235). Both are still recognisably Olive.
  • Built for every sizeFull-colour, cream-background and one-colour versions, a horizontal lockup, and a simplified favicon that stays legible at 16 pixels.

Typefaces

All are free Google Fonts, so anyone making a poster in Canva or Word can use the same type.

Libre Caslon

Headlines, titles and the school name. Set it upright and regular; never bold or italic. Caslon has been the face of British printing for nearly 300 years.

Libre Franklin

Body text, and small labels and buttons in spaced capitals (SemiBold, 0.14–0.18em letter-spacing).

Fraunces

Only the lettering on the crest ribbon. Don't use it for anything else.

Using the crest

Do

  • Leave clear space around the crest at least the height of the crown.
  • Use the full crest at 120 px wide or larger; below that, use the shield-only mark or favicon.
  • Use the SVG files wherever you can: they stay sharp at any size.
  • Use the one-colour versions for embroidery on uniforms and for rubber stamps.

Don't

  • Add phone numbers, slogans or effects (shadows, glows, outlines) to the crest.
  • Stretch, rotate or recolour it outside the versions above.
  • Put the gold crest on cream or white; use the light version there.
  • Redraw or retype the ribbon text. Ask for a new version instead.
